Engram is licensed under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or later** (AGPL-3.0-or-later). See [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) for the full text.

## What this means in practice

- **Personal / self-hosted use** — no obligations beyond keeping the copyright notice. You can use Engram for yourself, modify it, share modified versions with friends, run it on your own server, integrate it into your own private workflows. AGPL does not interfere.
- **Internal use within an organization** — same: no source-disclosure obligation as long as the modified version stays inside the organization and is not exposed as a network service to outside users.
- **Public deployment as a hosted / SaaS service** — if you run a modified version of Engram and let users interact with it over a network, you must offer those users access to the corresponding source code (AGPL §13). This applies to the modified version, not just to your additional code wrapped around an unmodified Engram.
- **Bundling with proprietary code** — the combined work falls under AGPL. You cannot ship Engram (or a derivative) inside a closed-source product.

If you are uncertain whether your use case requires source disclosure, **read AGPL-3.0 §13 directly** or consult counsel — the conditions are precise and not paraphrased here.

## Commercial / closed-source license

If your use case requires AGPL-incompatible terms — typically:

- bundling Engram inside a closed-source product
- deploying a modified Engram as a hosted service without releasing the modifications
- redistributing under terms other than AGPL

a separate commercial license is available. Contact the maintainer to discuss terms.

This dual-licensing arrangement is intentional: AGPL keeps the project firmly open for individual users and the open-source ecosystem, while the commercial license allows companies that need different terms to obtain them through a direct relationship rather than ignoring the license.
